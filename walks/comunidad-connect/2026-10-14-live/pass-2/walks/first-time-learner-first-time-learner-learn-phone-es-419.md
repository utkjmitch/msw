# Marisol — first-time-learner-learn (phone, es-419)

**Reached my goal:** yes. In under two minutes, in Spanish, I learned what they do and where, and found two ways to take part without giving money. I never had to give an email.

## What I tried
The site opened in Spanish on its own, which felt nice. A cookie box covered half the screen right away, but it was in Spanish and had a clear "Rechazar todo" button, so I tapped that. The big headline, "Transformando Vidas Juntos", is vague. The sentence under it is not: health, housing, youth development and cultural connections in Nicaragua, Dominican Republic and the United States. I tapped "Involúcrate" and scrolled through the cards. I found "Viajar" (join a trip or organize one for your group), "Donar", "Asociarse" (for institutions, not me) and "Compartir Historias". Under the cards there's a newsletter sign-up, but nobody pushed it on me before I could read.

## Where I struggled
- In the story strip on the Spanish home page, the titles are in English: "How We're Born", "July 2026 Bulletin", "Where We're Born". They sit right next to "Leer la historia". It's small, but it's the mix I worry about.
- The "Involúcrate" intro says "ser voluntario", but no volunteer card ever shows up. That's odd, since the reason I'm here is a photo of volunteers painting a classroom.
- A lot of "impacto" and "agentes de cambio" without concrete examples.

## What I'd tell a friend
"They work on health, housing and youth programs in Nicaragua and the Dominican Republic, plus some in the US. You can go on one of their trips or share a story. You don't have to donate to get involved."
