# The skills: built, followed, fixed

*Date: 2026-11-04. Branch: `mfx/msw-skills`, from `main` at `6dcd4b1`. Model: claude-opus-5-5. The proof agents ran on claude-sonnet-5-5.*

## What was asked

Jimmy asked for the skills another team can follow, in its own repo, to write its own Walkers and walk its own product. Each skill had to be followed start to finish against the repo's own example site, and every place a follower got stuck had to be fixed and recorded here. Later the same day he added two things. Every persona run must include silly, weird and edge walks. Every run must also look for blind spots: what the requester assumed, who they didn't mention, which situations nobody asked about.

## What was made

Six skills in `skills/`, each a plain `SKILL.md`, with the spec's names (section 6) where they match:

| Goal's name | Skill | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `write-walker` | [`msw-dossier`](../../skills/msw-dossier/SKILL.md) | Interview whoever knows the users; write full Walkers, edge and unusual ones included; keep the product's own words out of them |
| `write-expert-walker` | [`msw-expert-dossier`](../../skills/msw-expert-dossier/SKILL.md) | An expert at a kind of organization or at one named organization. A role, never a real person. Facts go in a fact sheet with their source and two dates. The owner chooses the sources: public records only, or the team's private material as well, which keeps the Walker in private storage. Output is labeled simulated. The expert's claims are marked "checked / not checked" |
| `write-journey` | [`msw-journeys`](../../skills/msw-journeys/SKILL.md) | A goal in the Walker's words, observable success, a budget, read-only rules, and a saved login prepared by a person |
| (added) | [`msw-silly`](../../skills/msw-silly/SKILL.md) | Silly walks from spec 6.2, using only what the server can do today, each with a `# Why:` line from the Walker's life |
| `walk` | [`msw-walk`](../../skills/msw-walk/SKILL.md) | Plan the run, blind-spot pass included, with edge and silly walks by default; set it up; walk it one walk at a time in a fresh session that hasn't seen the code; page text untrusted; leave when the Walker would |
| `file-findings` | [`msw-file`](../../skills/msw-file/SKILL.md) | Ask the owner where findings go and offer the guide's four options; record the answer; put a README beside the records; write a short report, marking every finding the walk didn't prove |

[`skills/README.md`](../../skills/README.md) gives the order and the rules every skill keeps.

Code, each piece with a test:

- `server/src/practice-site.ts` (`node server/dist/practice-site.js`): serves the example site in `server/tests/fixtures/site/` at `http://127.0.0.1:4810/`, the address the examples already use. Until now nothing served it outside the tests. Tests: `server/tests/practice-site.test.ts`.

Docs:

- `README.md`: the status now says the skills are written, and points at them.
- `docs/using-in-a-private-project.md`: a section on the skills and their order, a pointer from the expert section to `msw-expert-dossier`, and a new section, **No route to GitHub**: a pinned copy built on a connected machine with `npm ci`, with Chromium build 1243 in place and `PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH` pointing at it. It also says the reports in option 4 can't live in a public repo, for the same reason as option 1.

What the skills don't use, because it doesn't exist yet: the Cheese Shop and its answer key, `msw report`, `msw compare`, `msw score`, `msw individuals` and `msw publish`. The report is written by hand from the walk folders. Practice walks use the small example site, which has no answer key, so nothing here measures how much the method finds. The spec's `msw-guide`, `msw-inspect` and `msw-vision` are not written.

## How it was proven

Each skill was followed by a fresh `claude -p` session (claude-sonnet-5-5). Each session got only the Ministry repo (a copy of this branch), a practice "product repo" and a file of owner answers. The product repo held the example site, presented as "the Fixture Trust", an invented savings trust. The owner answers were written as a made-up web lead, "Sam". Every session wrote down what it made, the output of every check it ran, and where the skill left it stuck. The skill was fixed after each session, before the next one ran. The proof folders lived in `/tmp` and are not committed: they hold a walk of a practice product, and the Ministry's repo keeps no walk records.

### The walks

Each walk ran in its own fresh session. The session got the server through `mcp.json`, plus `msw-walk`, the Walker's dossier, the journey and its row of the plan. It was not allowed to read the site's code. Every walk sealed with `verified: true` and `alive: true`, and every Walker finding reproduced on replay.

| # | Walker, journey, viewport, locale | Kind | Outcome | Steps | Findings (Walker + server) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | fund-giver, fund-giver-silly-double-tap, phone, en-US | silly | run-away: Donate pressed twice and nothing visible happened | 2 of 15 | 2 + 1 |
| 9 | first-timer, first-timer-learn, phone, en-US | ordinary | run-away: two screens in a row that don't say what the Trust does | 1 of 20 | 3 + 5 |
| 10 | compliance-officer, compliance-check, desktop, en-US | edge, expert | run-away: no owner, regulator, complaints route or address | 3 of 30 | 3 + 5 |
| 11 | first-timer, first-timer-silly-large-text, phone, en-US | silly | run-away: buttons stay tiny at 200 percent; nothing says what the Trust is | 1 of 20 | 4 + 5 |
| 12 | second-language, second-language-learn, phone, es-US | edge | run-away: no Spanish anywhere, and a name asked for first | 3 of 30 | 2 + 5 |

The double tap was a real silly walk, not a description of one. Walk 8 typed a name, then pressed Donate twice in one move (`how: "double-tap"`). Its `events.jsonl` holds two `blocked` events, `would-submit` to `/donate`, 81 ms apart. The second has `fields_error`, because the second press didn't come from the form's own submit control. So the page did nothing to stop a second submission. A separate probe script, run directly against the server, gave the same two events. Walk 11 ran a second silly walk, with text at 200 percent.

Four of the nine planned walks were not walked: the ordinary fund-giver walk on both viewports, the first-timer walk on desktop, and the accents silly walk. They were left out to keep the practice short. The practice report lists them as gaps, as `msw-file` now asks.

The example site is a test fixture: pages built to exercise the server, with almost no content, a broken image and an unlabeled button. So every Walker left within three steps. That is the right result for this site, but it means the walks never ran the patience out or the step budget dry. Those paths were tested only by the server's own tests.

### The report

The `msw-file` part 2 session wrote `REPORT.md` for the practice run: 134 lines in the eight sections the skill asks for. It covers the summary table; top findings, each marked proven or with why it is not, with event and screenshot evidence; the two silly walks; a blind-spot table covering every item in the plan, empty ones included; each Walker; the expert's claims, all `not checked` and labeled simulated; run health; and the storage choice (option 1, Sam, 2026-11-04). Its opening paragraph names three things to do first. Its eleven stuck points are the rows marked 13 in the table below, and all were fixed in `msw-file` after it ran. The report itself stays in `/tmp` with the rest of the practice product.

## Findings about the server

These came up while walking. Neither is a skill problem, and the server is outside this lane, so neither was changed.

1. **Typing into a field costs a point of patience.** The seal of walk 8 lists `no-change` after the act that typed a name. The cause is in `server/src/browser/perceive.ts:35`: the page state hashes the address, the visible text and the options, but not the values typed into fields. So a typed name leaves the state unchanged, and `server/src/walk/session.ts:375` charges `no-change`. A blocked read-only submit is charged the same way. `msw-walk` now tells the Walker about both charges, so nobody mistakes them for a Walker's own choice. Spec 5.4 charges for "an action that changes nothing visible", and typed text is visible. The decision on whether to fix it is below.
2. **`text_scale` works, and a page without a viewport tag looks unscaled.** In walk 11 the About page looked normal-sized at 200 percent. It isn't a scaling failure. `about.html` has no `<meta name="viewport">`, so a phone lays it out 980 px wide and shrinks it. The "About" heading measures about 26 px in `screens/14.png`: twice the 32 px default heading at 200 percent, times the 0.4 shrink. The Walker's finding (tiny text on the About page) is a real phone problem with that page.
